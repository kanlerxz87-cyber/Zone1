## 5. US law

### 5.1 S9 — 16 CFR Part 255 (eCFR)

`https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255` — page header:
*"Displaying title 16, up to date as of 11/12/2026. Title 16 was last amended 10/30/2026."*,
source line `88 FR 48102, July 26, 2023`.

**§ 255.5(a)** read verbatim: *"When there exists a connection between the endorser and the seller
of the advertised product that might materially affect the weight or credibility of the
endorsement, and that connection is not reasonably expected by the audience, such connection must
be disclosed clearly and conspicuously …"*

**§ 255.5(c)** (partially read) defines *clear and conspicuous*: *"A disclosure is not clear and
conspicuous if a consumer must take any action, such as clicking on a hyperlink or hovering over
an icon, to see it."* → the basis for the site's always-visible disclosure bar rather than a
hover-revealed one.

### 5.2 16 CFR Part 465 (eCFR)

`https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465` — source line
`89 FR 68077, Aug. 22, 2024`.

**§ 465.1(c)** *"Clear and conspicuous"* read verbatim, including *"(4) In any communication using an
interactive electronic medium, such as social media or the internet, the disclosure must be
unavoidable."*

**§ 465.6 Company-controlled review websites or entities** read verbatim: *"It is an unfair or
deceptive act and practice and a violation of this part for a business to materially misrepresent,
expressly or by implication, that a website, organization, or entity that it controls, owns, or
operates provides independent reviews or opinions, other than consumer reviews, about a category
of businesses, products, or services including the business or one or more of the products or
services it sells."*

→ This is the clause behind SG-03 ("stop calling the site independent").
