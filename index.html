Start with the decision, not the products

If you cook two different things at once

The dual-basket case

Two independent 3-quart baskets, each on its own temperature and timer, with a Sync Finish function that holds the quicker basket so both land together. This is the form factor that solves the one real complaint about air fryers: you cannot cook chips and chicken at 200 °C and 180 °C in one drawer at the same time.

If you want an oven, not a drawer

The French-door case

A 26-quart cavity that opens from the front, with a rotisserie and 24 named presets in the manual. It bakes, toasts and air-fries where a basket only air-fries — and it takes up the counter space of a small microwave rather than fitting in a cupboard.

If a number decided it for you

The spec-sheet case

Capacity in quarts is not capacity in litres, and "450 °F" can mean the maximum setting or a two-minute finishing burst. The single most useful thing you can do before buying any air fryer is learn which of its numbers are comparable.
