What has to be decided before more is built

Three decisions, one measurement plan, and the short list of things only the account holder can do. Nothing on this page is implemented: it exists so the paths can be compared before anyone spends time on one.

Decision 1 — which niche to build around

The site already covers the three supplied products. The strategic question is which reader intent to build around them, because that decides article topics, internal links and the query each page is written for. Four candidates are scored on evidence this project actually holds; a fifth column names what would have to be true for each.

What each criterion means
  • C1 Documented by official sources — The only kind of differentiation this project can publish without inventing experience.
  • C2 Matches a supplied product — Traffic is worthless here if it cannot be served by one of the three links.
  • C3 Repeatable topic supply — A niche needs more than three articles to satisfy the 10-post expectation and to earn internal links.
  • C4 Competitive room for a small site — Broad 'best air fryer' queries are dominated by publishers with test kitchens; the site claims none.
  • C5 Policy safety — Price tracking, review aggregation and unverified testing claims are excluded by Amazon policy or the FTC rules.
Recommendation: Build on N1, with N2 as the internal-link cluster and N3 as the trust layer. Do not buy traffic against N4 until the operator has real testing.

N1 is the only candidate where all three supplied products are equally useful, every claim is already sourced, and the site's existing articles already point at it (live count on the articles index).

Alternative: If the operator wants to grow the audience before the products, start with N3 and accept slower conversion; the measurement page explains how to tell the difference.

Decision 2 — how traffic would actually arrive

Search is the only channel this project can describe without an account, a budget or a social following. The plan below is ordered by 'how much does it depend on things we do not have' — least dependent first.

T1

Search — long-tail format questions

Ready to execute once the tag and account exist

Each article answers one question a buyer types before they know which product they want ('dual basket vs single basket', 'what does 450F mean'). Competition on long-tail questions is materially lower than on 'best air fryer'.

Requires

  • a live domain
  • an approved Associates account with tagged links (S2, S4)
  • sitemap submitted to Google Search Console by the owner — this repo cannot do it

Effort: Medium · Cost: Domain (annual) — price not verified in this project, so not stated · Risk: 6–12 weeks before meaningful impressions on a new domain; unverifiable from here

T2

Search — specification and manual questions

Articles already exist in this shape (live count on the articles index)

Owner's manuals are public PDFs and questions about them are specific ('RJ38-SQSS-8T temperature range'). A page that quotes the manual can rank even on a small domain, and the audience is already holding the appliance or deciding on it.

Requires

  • the same as T1
  • the article guard already prevents invented numbers

Effort: Low · Cost: None beyond hosting · Risk: Low volume per page, so it needs breadth

T3

Search — 'is X worth it / what does Y mean' explainers

Two explainers exist; the new pass adds four more

The AI-slop tell is a page that repeats the product title. A page that answers a question the listing does not answer (what 450°F Hi-Fry actually does to the last two minutes of a cook, quoted from the manual and the brand page) is differently useful.

Requires

  • editorial judgement
  • the disclosure bar already on every page (S5, S8)

Effort: Low · Cost: None · Risk: Explainer traffic converts worse than comparison traffic; pair every explainer with a comparison link

T4

Pinterest — format and dimension cards

Blocked on original imagery

Kitchen-organization queries are visual and Pinterest sends durable outbound clicks. Dimension diagrams are honest assets the site can actually produce from published numbers.

Requires

  • a Pinterest account
  • an image pipeline
  • the site's own photography or diagrams

Effort: Medium · Cost: Time · Risk: Requires original imagery this repo does not have; using manufacturer images needs care under the IP License (S2)

T5

YouTube / Shorts — hands-on format comparisons

Not available to this project

It is the highest-trust channel for appliance buying, which is exactly why the site cannot fake it.

Requires

  • physical units
  • someone on camera
  • editing

Effort: High · Cost: Product cost + time · Risk: High; also the only channel where testing claims would become true

T6

Email newsletter

Sequence after T1–T3

It is the only channel the site owns outright and is unaffected by a search update.

Requires

  • a list — which requires traffic first
  • an email provider account

Effort: Low · Cost: Provider fee — not verified here, so not stated · Risk: Chicken and egg; do not start with it

Rules that keep the writing from reading like machine output

  1. Never open with 'In today's fast-paced world' or any variant. The new articles in this pass are checked against a banned-phrase list in build.py.
  2. Every number in an article must already exist in data/products.json; the build fails otherwise.
  3. No article may claim testing, ownership, or 'after weeks of use'.
  4. No 'best of' list unless it is a stated decision rule, not a ranking.
  5. One idea per section heading, phrased as the question a reader types.
  6. Say what is not known. A visible gap is more convincing than a confident guess, and it is the site's actual differentiator.
  7. No superlatives that an official source does not make ('the crispiest', 'the most powerful').

Decision 3 — what to measure

Nothing can be measured today: there is no tag on the supplied links, no analytics on the site, and no Associates report access. That is a design choice that protects the disclosure/policy position, and it is also the reason the brief's 'track clicks and conversions' feature cannot be built honestly yet. When to kill a direction

Give any new page cluster one full quarter. If a cluster has not produced a single outbound click by then, stop writing in it and reinvest the time in the cluster that has. Record the decision here so it is not relitigated from memory.

Without a pre-committed rule, every underperforming page looks like it 'just needs more time' and the site drifts back towards volume, which is what produced the AI-slop problem the brief calls out.

Prices, ratings and availability are not tracked and will not be: Program Policies §6(y) prohibits price-tracking or price-alerting functionality unless Amazon agrees, and §2(b) restricts price display. A build guard in build.py fails the build if such a component is added.

Privacy, cookies & local-only decision storage

This site sets no cookies itself, runs no analytics, and has no public accounts, comment forms or email-signup form. The analytics client is implemented but is not loaded: no production collector is deployed. No production event counts have been measured by this site.

The owner-only decision picker stores choices in this browser’s local storage. It sends nothing automatically; choices leave the page only when explicitly copied or downloaded. Use its clear button to remove the saved choices.

Hosting is separate: GitHub states that Pages logs visitors’ IP addresses for security. Any future analytics host or proxy also needs its own logging and retention review. Following an Amazon link takes you to Amazon, where Amazon’s privacy notice and cookie practices apply.
