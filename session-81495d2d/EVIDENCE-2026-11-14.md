**What this establishes.** The mapping is reproducible, not random: four independent sessions over two days, each
with distinct Amazon session ids and distinct `srctok` values, obtained exactly the same three ASINs; and the
resolver answers a recognised token with a product page while it answers an unrecognised token with 403. F-07's
clause (b) — "the same token returns a different featured product on a later request" — could not be reproduced
and is retired on this evidence. The three links in the brief are therefore treated as documenting B0CDY277D3,
B09B7SB46R and B08DKYBTPH.

**What this does not establish, and the site does not claim.**

* The supplied tokens are **not** ASINs — they are 9 characters where an ASIN is 10, and each 404s when pasted
  into `/dp/` (row 6). Only the `link.amazon` resolver accepts them.
* Every landing URL still carries `lv=shuf` and `plpRedirect=mhFallback`. That is Amazon's fallback handler, not a
  canonical tagged Associates URL. Whether an Associates tag is attached, and whose tag it is, cannot be seen from
  outside Associates Central — that stays open (flag F6 on the site).
* Who created the token→product mapping is unpublished, so the operator must still confirm these are the three
  products they meant (flag F1/F-01 on the site).

Operational instruction that follows from this: before any traffic, replace the three `link.amazon` links with
`https://www.amazon.com/dp/<ASIN>?tag=<your-tracking-id>` links generated inside Associates Central.
