What “450 °F” actually means on an air-fryer listing

A temperature badge may describe a brief finishing mode rather than the setting for a whole cook. The model code and manual matter more than the badge.

Read the time limit beside the temperature

The dual-window Chefman page publishes 200–450 °F / 95–235 °C. Its RJ38-SQPF-3TDB-2W manual describes Hi-Fry more narrowly: 450 °F / 235 °C, held for 2 minutes, for extra browning.

That instruction supports a short finishing boost. It is not evidence that the normal cooking controls hold that temperature for an entire cycle.

Two 8-quart pages do not describe one interchangeable model

The stainless RJ38-SQSS-8T is documented at 200–400 °F. Chefman separately lists RJ38-8T-BLACK with a Hi-Fry button for the final two minutes and four named cooking functions.

A feature on the second page cannot be transferred to the first because their names are similar. Nor does the existence of both pages establish which unit an unconfirmed Amazon listing supplies.

What the listing conflict does—and does not—prove

The dated listing observation in this repository describes a stainless 8-quart unit with a 450 °F Hi-Fry option. It does not settle the relationship to the two manufacturer models. A stale title, a variant difference or another explanation remains unconfirmed; this site assigns no probability to them.

Before buying

Find the selected variant and model number, then compare them with the manufacturer page and matching manual. Ask the seller or manufacturer if they disagree. If the feature is essential and the identity cannot be confirmed, do not rely on the badge alone. Higher temperature by itself is not a tested browning result.
