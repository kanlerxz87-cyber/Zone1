What "450 °F" actually means on an air fryer listing

On one Chefman model it is a two-minute finishing blast. On another it is a setting the manual never mentions. The badge is the same. The feature is not.

Three different things get called "450 °F"

A full-cycle ceiling. The Chefman 6 Qt documents a 200–450 °F / 95–235 °C working range, with Hi-Fry documented as 450 °F / 235 °C, held for 2 minutes, for extra browning.

A finishing blast on a lower-ceiling unit. Chefman's 8 Qt High Fry model is a different SKU from the stainless one this site maps: it publishes a Hi-Fry temperature of 450 °F / 232 °C and a four-function list alongside it, so the two are documented as separate things on the same page. See flag F33.

A marketing claim with no matching manual page. This is the case the site flags rather than resolves: the Amazon listing for the stainless 8-quart unit advertises a 450 °F Hi-Fry option, but the brand page for the model the site maps to that listing documents a 200–400 °F range and four food presets.

Why the badge matters commercially

A higher ceiling is the difference between browning and steaming. But a "450 °F" badge can mean a full-cycle setting, a two-minute finish mode, or a number a copywriter typed. The only way to tell is the manual that ships with the unit — and for a listing whose model is not stated, that manual is the only document that is definitely about your machine.

Sources for this section: S12 S13

What this site does about it, and what it refuses to do

It publishes both claims, side by side, with a link to each, and asserts neither. It also publishes the fact that a second Chefman 8-quart SKU exists whose documented Hi-Fry and function list match the Amazon title exactly — which makes "one of these pages is stale" the less likely explanation and "these are two different units" the more likely one.

It does not pick a winner. A fabricated resolution would be worse than a visible conflict, because you would buy on the strength of it.

Sources for this section: S12 S26 S16

How to check in thirty seconds

Open the listing. Find the model number in the product-information block. Search that exact model on the manufacturer's site. Read the specification block on that page, not the bullet points in the carousel. If the model number is absent — which it is on all three listings in this set — you are relying on the brand's word about a unit you cannot identify, and you should say so to yourself before you check out.
