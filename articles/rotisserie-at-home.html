Rotisserie at home: what the 26 QT's manual actually documents

The preset and the spit set are in the manual. What the manual never claims — cooked weights, times, doneness — is the part worth reading before you plan a chicken dinner.

What is documented

The published spec row is deliberately narrow: Yes (manual documents a rotisserie preset and spit set) That wording summarizes two documents without stretching either: the brand page's feature list, which shows a Rotisserie section, and the owner's manual, whose preset chart lists Rotisserie by name and whose parts list includes the spit.

The rotisserie preset is one of the 24 presets are named in the owner's manual for model FAFO-001: Airfry, Fries, Bacon, Grill, Eggs, Fish, Ribs, Defrost, Steak, Vegetables, Wings, Bake, Rotisserie, Toast, Chicken, Pizza, Pastry, Proof, Broil, Slow Cook, Roast, Dehydrate, Reheat, Warm The count itself has a history: the brand's spec block says "25 Cooking Pre-sets" while the same page's description says "24 one-touch cooking presets" — the manual is treated as the controlling document here.

The spit is one of the 7 removable accessories counted from the manual's parts list, and the manual immediately concedes: "Some accessories may not be included with purchase."

Sources for this section: S14 S15

The machine the spit turns inside

The cavity is 26 qt / 24.6 L (manual: 1,519 cu in) — the reason a whole bird is plausible on paper. The doors are french doors, single-pull opening; the air system is dual-speed fan, 360° air circulation. The oven runs on 120 V / 60 Hz at 1700 W, with a documented range of 75–500 °F / 25–260 °C.

Numbers the documents do not publish: a maximum rotisserie weight, a suggested time per pound, a doneness temperature. None of those appears on the brand page or in the manual, so none appears here. Judgement: a recipe that needs them will come from a cookbook or a tested source, not from this spec sheet.

Sources for this section: S14 S15

The counting problem, stated plainly

The same brand page markets this oven as "10-in-1" in its feature headings while listing 24–25 presets elsewhere. Two counting systems, neither defined, on one page. This site's position is boring on purpose: report the manual's named presets, ignore the marketing arithmetic, and record the disagreement in the flag register so a reader can check it.

No unit was tested for this article. Everything above is a description of documents — which is exactly as far as a documentation-based site can honestly go on a feature like rotisserie.
