Why this site looks like this

Why this affiliate site shows no Amazon prices, ratings or review counts

It is not an oversight and it is not humility. Amazon's Program Policies only permit those numbers under two specific conditions, and this site meets neither.

Zone1 Kitchen · AI-assisted· Policy explainer· 4 min read· sources read 2026-11-14· Documentation-based — no hands-on testing Cluster: Can this page be trusted with my money?
No hands-on testing, and no Amazon numbers

Nobody here cooked with these appliances. Every specification below is quoted from the manufacturer's own product page or owner's manual and links to it. No Amazon price, star rating or review count appears, because Amazon's Program Policies only permit those when Amazon serves the link or via Creators/PA API S2.

The rule, quoted

From the Associates Program Participation Requirements, section 2(b): prices and availability may be shown only if "(a) we serve the link in which that price and availability data are displayed, or (b) you obtain Product pricing and availability data via Creators API or PA API" and comply with the License requirements.

And from section 6(t): you may not display customer reviews or star ratings "unless you have obtained a link to that customer review or star rating through Creators API or PA API".

This site is a static set of pages generated from a JSON file. Amazon does not serve its links here, and there are no Creators or PA-API credentials behind it. Both conditions fail, so both categories of data are absent.

There is a third clause in the same paragraph, and it is aimed squarely at comparison pages. If you display prices for a product in any “comparison” format together with prices for the same or similar products offered through other means, “you must display both the lowest ‘new’ price and, if we provide it to you, the lowest ‘used’ price at which the Product is available on the Amazon Site”. That is why the comparison matrix on this site has no price column at all — a half-comparison is precisely what the clause is aimed at.

Sources for this section: S2

The second rule people miss: no price tracking

Section 6(y) is blunt: "Unless otherwise agreed by Amazon, your Site must not have price tracking and/or price alerting functionality."

That rules out the most common affiliate feature of all — a price-history chart, a "price drop" alert, a "lowest ever" badge. A site that shows a price it scraped yesterday is not just out of date; it is the thing the clause is aimed at.

Sources for this section: S2

What a compliant site can honestly show instead

Manufacturer list prices, clearly labelled as the brand's own list price rather than a retailer price. Physical specifications, because the manufacturer owns those facts and they do not change by the hour. Commission rates from Amazon's own published table. And the gaps, named.

A number that is missing on purpose is a different object from a number that is missing by neglect. The first one a reader can trust the rest of the page around.

Sources for this section: S3 S10

The disclosure that does appear on every page

Operating Agreement section 5 requires the Associate to "clearly and prominently state" the identification "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases." — that sentence sits in the bar at the top of every page in this build, and the build fails if it is missing from any page.

The FTC's rule is the other half. A material connection must be disclosed "clearly and conspicuously" (16 CFR §255.5(a)), and the FTC's own guidance says the disclosure has to be placed with the endorsement rather than on a separate about page. Hence the persistent bar rather than a link in the footer.

Privacy & site technologies +

This site sets no cookies itself, runs no analytics, and has no comment forms or accounts. This GitHub Pages build sends no analytics events: the proposed first-party collector is not deployed and browser collection remains disabled. Following an Amazon link takes you to Amazon, whose own privacy notice and cookie practices apply from that point. No data you enter on reader pages is collected. The owner-only decision picker stores drafts in your own browser, only when used; it does not submit them. GitHub Pages may retain connection logs for security under its own privacy policy.

Amazon's Program Policies §3(e) require an accurate statement of cookie and third-party technology use on Associate sites. This notice describes this build; it is not a legal-compliance determination and must be reviewed the moment any analytics, embeds or affiliate-network script is added. The outbound-click counter for this site is written and shipped in assets/analytics.js but is switched off, because no endpoint host has been chosen — no analytics request is sent. Hosting still entails connection metadata. The paragraph above is generated from that switch, so it cannot describe a behaviour the page does not have.
