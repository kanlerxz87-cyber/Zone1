How the money works

Who pays for the air-fryer review you are reading?

Affiliate links are not a scandal and they are not invisible. Here is exactly how this site earns, what Amazon requires it to say, and the three claims it is not allowed to make.

By Zone1 Kitchen · AI-assisted document research· 4 min read· sources read 2026-11-14· Documentation-based — no hands-on testing Cluster: Can this page be trusted with my money?

No appliance was tested for this guide. Specifications are attributed to their documents; editorial guidance is not a cooking result. How we research.

The disclosure is a contract term, not a courtesy

Amazon's own guidance is precise about the wording: the Operating Agreement requires that the statement “As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.” clearly and conspicuously appears on the site. That is why the sentence sits above the header on every page here rather than in a footer nobody scrolls to.

The same page adds the link-level rule: the disclosure next to a link must be clear — examples given are '(paid link)', '#ad' or '#CommissionsEarned' — and conspicuous, placed near the link rather than somewhere a reader has to hunt for it.

Sources for this section: S5

US law adds a second, independent requirement

The FTC's influencer guidance says to disclose any financial relationship with a brand, to place the disclosure so it is hard to miss, and to use simple language. It also states plainly that the disclosure should sit with the endorsement itself, not only on a profile page or at the end of a post.

The binding rule behind that guidance is 16 CFR Part 255, whose §255.5 is titled 'Disclosure of material connections'.

Sources for this section: S8 S9

The three claims an affiliate site may not make

First, it may not claim hands-on experience it does not have — the FTC's own line is that you cannot talk about your experience with a product you have not tried. Every article on this site therefore states that nothing was tested.

Second, it may not publish Amazon prices, availability, star ratings or customer reviews unless Amazon serves the content or it comes through the official API. That is why every price on this site is either a manufacturer's own list price or blank.

Third, it may not run price-tracking or price-alert functionality unless Amazon agrees to it. A build check in this repository fails the build if such a component is added.

Sources for this section: S8 S2 S3

What that leaves for a site like this one

It leaves documentation. Capacity, dimensions, temperature ranges, basket counts, door types and warranty terms are all published by the manufacturer, all verifiable by a reader in two clicks, and none of them require the author to have switched the appliance on.

That is a narrower promise than most affiliate sites make, and it is the only one that can be kept without testing.
