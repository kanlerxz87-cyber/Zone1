Care, documented

The aerosol-oil caution nobody reads, quoted instead of paraphrased

Chefman's dual-window manual carries one CAUTION about spray cans that decides whether a nonstick basket keeps its coating. The sentence is short enough to quote whole, so this page does.

The sentence, in the manual's own words

The dual-window model's manual puts the rule inside the operating instructions, between a CAUTION marker and the step about loading food: “Never spray the baskets or racks with cooking oil from an aerosol spray can, which includes harsh components that can cause chipping of the nonstick coating. However, you can oil the racks or spray them with oil from a non-aerosol mister.”

The dataset publishes the same rule in its condensed form: Nonstick, dishwasher-safe baskets. Manual caution: never spray with aerosol cooking oil — use a non-aerosol mister instead. Both point at the same page; the manual is the primary document and is linked under this section.

Sources for this section: S18 S10

What the caution says, and what it leaves out

It names a cause and an effect — aerosol spray cans, harsh components, chipping of the nonstick coating — and it offers the substitute in the same breath: oil the racks, or spray them, from a non-aerosol mister. It does not set out a general oil rule; the manual handles that separately, telling the reader to toss food with a little oil in a separate bowl rather than filling the baskets.

The cleaning section carries the rest of the care story for the same model: the baskets and racks are top-rack dishwasher safe, the unit itself is wiped with a damp cloth and never submerged, and abrasive cleansers are off the list because they can damage the finishes.

Sources for this section: S18

The other model's care row, stated exactly as recorded

For the 8 Qt TurboFry Touch, this site's dataset records a shorter care row: Nonstick basket and rack; top-rack dishwasher safe. No aerosol rule is recorded for that model in this dataset, and this article quotes the caution only where the dataset carries it. If the second manual ever needs the same warning quoted, the source document is linked and the row can be checked against it.

That restraint is deliberate. A care instruction softened in retelling — the caution becoming “be careful with sprays”, the rule becoming optional — stops doing its job. Quoting the manufacturer's sentence keeps the obligation exact, and it gives the reader a link to the document that wrote it.
