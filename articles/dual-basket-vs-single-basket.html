Dual-basket vs single-basket air fryers: the one question that decides it

Capacity is the number everyone compares. It is not the one that decides anything. The question that matters is whether you need to cook two things at once, at different temperatures, and have them finish together.

Published 2026-11-14 · 6 min read · target query dual basket vs single basket air fryer · tags: dual basket air fryer, sync finish, air fryer comparison

What this article is built on: S10 S12 S13 S18
Every factual statement below carries a source badge linking to the official page or owner's manual it was read from. Nothing here was tested; no result is promised.

The question to ask first

Do you regularly need two different foods cooking at two different temperatures, at the same time, ready at the same minute? If yes, a dual-basket machine is the only form factor that answers it. If no, a single basket is simpler to wash, simpler to store, and cheaper to replace.

That is the whole decision. Everything else — quarts, watts, presets — is a detail that only matters once you have answered it.

What the dual-basket machine actually does

The Chefman 6 Qt is documented as a 6-quart unit built from two 3-quart baskets S10 S18, and its brand page lists both a Sync Function and two independent baskets S10. The manual explains two different sync behaviours, and the distinction is the useful part:

Sync Baskets is a shortcut — "The SYNC BASKETS button is a shortcut for setting both baskets to cook simultaneously with the same time and temperature" S18. Same food, same settings, both baskets.

Sync Finish is the interesting one — it "delays the start of the cooking cycle for the basket with the shorter cook time so that both baskets finish cooking at the same time" S18. Two different foods, two different temperatures, one finish time. That is the feature dual-basket machines actually exist for.

The manual is precise about what breaks the sync: pressing HI-FRY or +2 MIN on one side breaks it, and manually adjusting one basket's time or temperature breaks it S18. Worth knowing before you plan a two-dish cook.

The trade-off is one more thing to wash

Dual-basket machines have two baskets, two racks and two handles. The brand page lists nonstick, dishwasher-safe baskets for the Chefman 6 Qt S10, and the manual adds a specific caution: never spray the baskets or racks with cooking oil from an aerosol spray can, "which includes harsh components that can cause chipping of the nonstick coating. However, you can oil the racks or spray them with oil from a non-aerosol mister" S18. Two baskets means twice the coating to look after, and it is the one line in that manual that decides whether the coating survives a year.

Who should buy which

Buy the dual-basket if your household eats protein and sides at the same time and you dislike reheating because the first side went cold while the second cooked. The Sync Finish function exists for exactly that dinner, and the manual documents it in detail S18.

Buy the single basket if you mostly cook one thing at a time, want the fewest parts to wash, or want the larger single cavity. The 8 Qt's brand page describes it as designed for 4–6 people S12 and it publishes four one-touch presets with their default times and temperatures S13.
