Two-basket or one

Two 3-quart drawers or one 8-quart basket: which actually feeds four people?

The total volume is nearly identical. The cooking is not, and the difference is what you do when the first batch finishes.

The arithmetic, from the manufacturer's own pages

The dual-basket unit is 6 Qt / 5.67 L as two 3-quart baskets. The single-basket unit is 8 qt (brand also sells a 5 qt version of the same product page), documented as designed for 4–6 people.

So the single basket holds more at once — a third more — and the two drawers hold less per drawer but can run independently. That is the whole trade, and everything else is detail.

What Sync Finish is actually for

Sync Finish delays the start of the shorter cook so both baskets finish together; a separate Sync Baskets function copies time and temperature to the second basket.

This matters because the failure mode of batch cooking is the second batch going cold while the first one waits. A machine that plans both drawers to land at the same moment solves a problem that a bigger single basket creates rather than solves.

Sources for this section: S10 S18

Where the single basket wins

One big batch of one thing. A whole chicken, a full tray of fries, a pile of wings for a crowd. Splitting 8 quarts into two 3-quart drawers means every basket-shaped item has to fit a 3-quart drawer.

The documented one-touch presets on the single-basket unit are fries, chicken, fish, meat (one-touch), and the manual publishes a default for each: Default 400 °F / 15 min · Meat 375 °F / 18 min · Chicken 375 °F / 22 min · Fish 350 °F / 10 min · Fries 400 °F / 20 min.

Sources for this section: S12 S13

The decision, stated as a decision

If most of your meals are two components that should finish together, the dual basket is the better tool and the capacity number is a red herring. If most of your meals are one component in quantity, the 8-quart is the better tool and the Sync Finish feature is a feature you would never press.

This is a judgement about how people cook, not a fact from a manufacturer page, and it is labelled as such.

Neither unit was tested. Capacity, presets, defaults and features are quoted from the brand's product pages and the owner's manual for the single-basket model.
