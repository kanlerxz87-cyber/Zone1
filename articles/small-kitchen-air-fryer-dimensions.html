Fit check

Will it fit? Start with dimensions, then read the clearance rules

A body fitting on a counter is not proof it can safely operate there. Keep the manufacturer’s dimensions and operating instructions together.

Write down the dimensions from the right variant

The dual-window Chefman is listed at 13.38 × 11.81 × 10.63 in / 33.98 × 30 × 27 cm. The 8-quart Chefman’s combined row is 8 Qt model: 15.4 × 12.6 × 13.5 in / 39.11 × 32 × 34.29 cm · 12.8 lb / 5.8 kg. The Emeril page lists 16.9 D × 15.8 W × 14.8 H in.

The Chefman page separates its 5-quart and 8-quart figures. Do not take one model’s measurements from the other variant’s row.

Sources for this section: S10 S12 S14

Add the operating space

The Chefman 8-quart manual calls for at least 6 inches of clearance on the top and back. The Emeril manual calls for at least 5 inches of free space around the top, back and sides and separately says not to operate under hanging cabinets.

These are different instructions for different units. A single clearance number cannot be substituted for the complete manual. Allow for access to drawers, doors, controls and the outlet as well.

Sources for this section: S13 S15 S18

Do not turn dimensions into a cooking-efficiency score

No useful “capacity per square inch” ranking follows without knowing the dimension order, usable food area and operating clearance. A nominal volume divided by a body footprint would not measure food capacity or performance.

Sources for this section: S10 S12 S14

Before you choose the spot

Check the exact unit’s manual, the surface requirements and any cabinet or counter restrictions. The repository records conflicting dimensions for the unconfirmed 8-quart listing; manufacturer figures do not resolve that listing identity.

Editorial judgement: leave the placement decision open if the model or measurements remain uncertain. This guide is not an electrical installation or countertop heat-resistance assessment.
