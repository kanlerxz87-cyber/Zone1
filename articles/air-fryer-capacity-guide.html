26 quarts, 1,519 cubic inches: an honest air fryer capacity guide

Air fryer capacity is quoted in quarts, and quarts are a cavity measurement rather than a food measurement. So this guide uses the numbers the brands print, shows you the arithmetic they imply, and is explicit about the one thing none of them publish.

Published 2026-11-14 · 6 min read · target query 26 quart air fryer capacity · tags: air fryer capacity, quart to litre, countertop oven size

What this article is built on: S10 S12 S13 S14 S15 S18

Do the arithmetic, because the brands make you

The Emeril is the one unit that publishes both a cubic-inch figure and a litre figure, which lets you check the arithmetic rather than take it on trust. The manual's specifications table reads: "FAFO-001 | 120 V/1700 W/60 Hz | 1700 W | 26 quarts (1,519 cubic inches) | 75° F - 500° F/ 25° C - 260° C | LED" S15.

Converting 1,519 cubic inches gives roughly 24.9 litres. The brand's product page states 24.6 Liters S14. These sit about 1% apart — close enough that both are consistent with the same cavity, and far enough that anyone quoting one to two decimal places should say which document it came from. The same page also lists "26-Quart Capacity" in its specification strip and describes the unit as "big enough to accommodate nearly all your cooking needs" S14.

Do the same check on the Chefman numbers. The 6 Qt's published 5.67 L and the 8 Qt's published 7.57 L are direct brand figures from the two product pages S10 S12 — no conversion needed, and no reason to distrust them.

The conversion is arithmetic, not a published figure

24.9 litres is our own conversion of the manual's 1,519 cubic inches, not a number any manufacturer published. The published figures are 26 quarts and 24.6 Liters. Where this site shows derived numbers, it says so.

The only household-size claim in this set — and why it is the only one

Exactly one official page in this product set maps a capacity to a number of people. Chefman's 8 Qt page says the basket is "Designed for 4-6 people" S12. No other unit here makes that mapping, and the Emeril page, which does name a large household context, stops at describing the cavity as spacious rather than assigning it to a family size S14.

That single claim is worth more than a page of folk maths, because it is a manufacturer telling you how it expects the product to be used. Everything else — the "feeds 6-8", "perfect for a family of four" lines that fill comparison blogs — is somebody's estimate built on a quart figure that was never a food volume to begin with.

One caution on the 8 Qt page specifically: it covers two products, the 5 qt and the 8 qt, and the capacity, dimensions and weight rows are given for both models S12. Check which model you are actually reading before you measure your shelf.

What nobody publishes: usable food volume

This is the number that would actually answer "will this feed my family", and not one of these three manufacturers publishes it. Usable volume is smaller than cavity volume because the heating element, the basket walls and the rack all take space, and how much depends on the food.

So this guide cannot tell you how many chicken wings fit, and neither can any other page that quotes only a quart figure. What it can tell you is the relative scale, which is a real decision:

The 8 Qt is a single cavity of 7.57 L that the brand says is designed for 4–6 people S12. The 6 Qt is the same class of cooking in two 3-quart baskets S10 S18 — the same nominal total, split so two dishes can finish together. The 26 QT is a different category entirely at 24.6 L S14, documented with a rotisserie, a crisper tray, a baking pan and a wire rack S15.

Any page that tells you the exact number of drumsticks will be guessing. This one will not.

Before you measure your counter

Capacity is the number you will argue about with other shoppers. Counter space is the number that will argue with your kitchen. The Emeril manual sets out its own ventilation requirements: at least 5 inches of free space on the top, back and sides, nothing on top of the appliance, and never under hanging cabinets S15. The unit measures 16.9″D × 15.8″W × 14.8″H S14.

The fryers need less: 13.38 × 11.81 × 10.63 in for the Chefman 6 Qt S10 and 15.4 × 12.6 × 13.5 in for the 8 Qt S12.

And if you are still choosing between them, the dual-basket question comes before capacity, and the toaster-oven question comes before both.
