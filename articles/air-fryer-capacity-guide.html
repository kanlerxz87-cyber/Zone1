Capacity, explained

26 quarts, 1,519 cubic inches: an honest air fryer capacity guide

Quart capacity is a cavity measurement, not a food measurement. So this guide uses the numbers the manufacturers print, shows the arithmetic they imply, and is explicit about the one thing none of them publishes: usable food volume.

No hands-on testing, and no Amazon numbers

Nobody here cooked with these appliances. Every specification below is quoted from the manufacturer's own product page or owner's manual and links to it. What the brands actually publish

Three units, three different ways of stating the same idea, all from official pages. The dual-window fryer is 6 Qt / 5.67 L. The single-basket fryer is sold in two sizes on one page, listed as 8 qt (brand also sells a 5 qt version of the same product page). The countertop oven is 26 qt / 24.6 L (manual: 1,519 cu in).

The oven is the only one that publishes a cubic-inch figure as well as a litre figure, which lets you check the arithmetic instead of taking it on trust.

Do the arithmetic, because the brands make you

The oven's owner's manual gives its specification table as model FAFO-001, 120 V/1700 W/60 Hz, rated power 1700 W, capacity 26 quarts (1,519 cubic inches), 75 °F - 500 °F, LED display. Converting 1,519 cubic inches gives roughly 24.9 litres. The brand's own page states 26 qt / 24.6 L (manual: 1,519 cu in).

Those two figures sit about one per cent apart — close enough that both describe the same cavity, and far enough apart that anyone quoting litres to two decimal places should say which document it came from. The two Chefman figures need no conversion at all: they are printed on the brand's own specification blocks.

The conversion is arithmetic, not a published figure. The published numbers are 26 quarts and 24.6 litres. Where this site shows a derived number, it says so.

Sources for this section: S14 S15

The only household-size claim in this set — and why it is the only one

Exactly one official page in this product set maps a capacity to a number of people: the single-basket fryer is designed for 4–6 people. No other unit here makes that mapping, and the oven's page, which does describe the cavity as spacious, stops short of assigning it to a family size.

That single claim is worth more than a page of folk maths, because it is a manufacturer telling you how it expects the product to be used. Everything else — the “feeds 6-8”, “perfect for a family of four” lines that fill comparison blogs — is somebody's estimate built on a quart figure that was never a food volume to begin with.

One caution on that page: it covers two products, the 5 qt and the 8 qt, and the capacity, dimensions and weight rows are given for both. Check which model you are reading before you measure your shelf.

Sources for this section: S12 S14

What nobody publishes: usable food volume

This is the number that would actually answer “will this feed my family”, and not one of these three manufacturers publishes it. Usable volume is smaller than cavity volume because the heating element, the basket walls and the rack all take space, and how much depends on the food.

So this guide cannot tell you how many chicken wings fit, and neither can any other page that quotes only a quart figure. What it can tell you is the relative scale, which is a real decision. The 8 qt is a single cavity of 7.57 L that the brand says is designed for 4–6 people. The 6 qt is the same class of cooking in two 3-quart baskets — the same nominal total, split so two dishes can finish together. The 26 qt is a different category entirely, documented with a rotisserie, a crisper tray, a baking pan and a wire rack.

Any page that tells you the exact number of drumsticks will be guessing. This one will not.

Before you measure your counter

Capacity is the number you will argue about with other shoppers. Counter space is the number that will argue with your kitchen. The oven's manual sets out its own ventilation requirements: leave at least 5 inches of free space on the top, back and sides, never put anything on top of the appliance, and never operate it under hanging cabinets. The unit measures 16.9 D × 15.8 W × 14.8 H in.

The fryers need less: 13.38 × 11.81 × 10.63 in / 33.98 × 30 × 27 cm for the dual-window unit, and 8 Qt model: 15.4 × 12.6 × 13.5 in / 39.11 × 32 × 34.29 cm · 12.8 lb / 5.8 kg for the single-basket one.

And if you are still choosing between them, the form-factor question comes before capacity, and the toaster-oven question comes before both.

No unit in this set publishes a usable food volume, and none has been tested by anyone who wrote this. Those gaps are real, and they are why this site refuses to declare a winner.
