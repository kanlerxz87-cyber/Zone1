Air fryer vs toaster oven: which one earns its counter space?

The shelf space argument is really an argument about what each machine is documented to do. One of these three appliances is a 24-preset countertop oven. The other two are fryers with one to five buttons. That difference shows up in your kitchen long before it shows up in a price comparison.

Published 2026-11-14 · 7 min read · target query air fryer vs toaster oven · tags: air fryer, toaster oven, countertop oven, buying guide

The short answer

If the word oven in the box is doing real work, you are not buying an air fryer. The 26-quart French-door unit in this set is documented in its owner's manual with 24 named cooking presets, and the list includes Toast, Pizza, Bake, Rotisserie, Broil, Slow Cook, Roast, Dehydrate, Reheat, Pastry and Proof alongside the fryer presets S15. That is an oven with an air-fryer mode attached — the manual's own heating-element chart splits its modes into Convection Oven, Dehydrate, Grill and Turbo Fan with Spiral Heating Element S15.

The two fryers are not competing with it. The Chefman 8 Qt is documented by its brand as 4 cooking presets on a 200–400 °F range S12, and its manual's preset chart is a single page of food types — Default, Meat, Chicken, Fish and Fries, each with a default time and temperature S13. The Chefman 6 Qt's manual documents HI-FRY, +2 MIN, SYNC BASKETS and SYNC FINISH as functions, and refers once to "a preset function" without publishing a preset list at all S18.

So the decision is not air fryer versus toaster oven. It is: do you want the smallest tool that air-fries well, or do you want to retire the toaster oven and the microwave's leftovers duty in one box?

Three different temperature ceilings — and only one of them is a full cycle

This is the number most comparison articles get wrong, so it is worth being precise. The Chefman 6 Qt's brand page gives a range of 200–450 °F S10, and its manual explains the top of that range as a two-minute boost: the HI-FRY button "will raise the cooking temperature to 450˚F (235˚C) for 2 minutes, to give foods a little additional browning" S18. The 450 °F is real, but it is a finishing move, not a setting you cook at.

The Chefman 8 Qt's brand page gives 200–400 °F and states "Max temp: 400ºF/ 205ºC" S12 — a plain, full-cycle ceiling.

The Emeril manual's specifications table gives 75 °F – 500 °F / 25 °C – 260 °C, with 120 V / 1700 W / 60 Hz and a rated power of 1700 W S15. Note that 75 °F is a low-temperature floor, useful for proofing and warming rather than for frying.

If your food needs 425 °F for more than two minutes, only two of these three machines can do it at all, and neither of those two is the one advertising 450 °F.

The clearance question nobody asks until the box is open

Counter-top ovens have ventilation requirements that fryers do not, and the Emeril manual states them plainly: leave at least 5 inches of free space on the top, back and sides and above the appliance, never put anything on top of it, never operate it under hanging cabinets, and never set it on a cooktop S15. The unit itself is 16.9″D × 15.8″W × 14.8″H S14.

The two fryers are smaller and simpler: 13.38 × 11.81 × 10.63 in and 13.3 lb for the Chefman 6 Qt S10, and 15.4 × 12.6 × 13.5 in and 12.8 lb for the 8 Qt S12.

Do the arithmetic against your own kitchen before the box is on the counter. An oven that needs five inches of clearance on every side and cannot go under a cabinet is not a small appliance.

What the paperwork does not tell you

No unit in this set publishes a usable food volume, and none has been tested by anyone who wrote this. Those gaps are real, and they are the reason this site refuses to declare a winner. If you need a capacity guide that works from published numbers, read the capacity guide — it uses the manufacturers' own quart and litre figures and nothing else.

There is also an unresolved conflict in this product set worth knowing about before you shop: the Chefman 8 Qt's Amazon listing advertises a 450 °F Hi-Fry option plus bake, dehydrate and frozen functions, while the brand's own page and manual document 200–400 °F with four food presets S12 S13 S16. Both official pages are live at the same time, saying different things. That contradiction is flagged here rather than smoothed over — and it is the reason the article below exists.

How to pick in one line

Buy a toaster-oven-style unit if you currently own a toaster oven and a microwave and want one box to do both jobs. Buy an air fryer if you want the smallest thing that crisps food well, and you are happy to use the oven you already have for toast and baking.
