Power, documented

1700 watts, three manuals, and one extension-cord rule that is not the same

All three appliances in this set carry the same wattage rating from their manufacturers. Their manuals do not agree on how to power them, and one of the three rules is absolute.

No hands-on testing, and no Amazon numbers

The same rating, three times

The Chefman 6 Qt dual-window model documents 1700 W / 120 V on its product page. The Chefman 8 Qt TurboFry Touch documents 1700 W / 120 V on its page as well. The Emeril Lagasse French Door AirFryer 360 documents a rated power of 1700 W on 120 V / 60 Hz.

The Emeril's specification table in its owner's manual states the same figures for model FAFO-001, and its heating-element section attributes the turbo-fan mode to a top spiral heating element at that same wattage. Nothing in this paragraph is an estimate; each figure is a published dataset value with the manufacturer's page behind it.

Sources for this section: S10 S12 S14 S15

The Chefman manuals: an extension cord is allowed, with one condition

Both Chefman manuals explain that the supplied cord is deliberately short, to reduce entanglement and tripping hazards. A longer detachable extension cord may be used if care is exercised, and both manuals attach the same condition: the marked electrical rating of the extension cord should be at least as great as the electrical rating of the appliance. The longer cord, they add, should be arranged so it will not drape over a countertop or tabletop where it can be pulled on or tripped over.

Before any of that, both manuals ask a question most buyers skip: check that the home power supply corresponds with the intended voltage of the appliance.

Sources for this section: S18 S13

The Emeril manual: the rule is absolute

The French Door AirFryer 360's manual states it differently, in its safeguards: never use with an extension cord. That line is the manufacturer's, not this site's, and it is the sharpest single instruction in this comparison.

The same safeguards section defines where the appliance may sit: a flat, heat-resistant surface, countertop use only, not in an enclosed space or under hanging cabinets, with free space left on the top, back and sides and above the appliance, and never on a cooktop.

Sources for this section: S15

What a buyer can take from three manuals

If one of these appliances is heading for a kitchen where the outlet is far from the counter, the manuals' difference matters before any feature does. Two manufacturers permit a rated extension cord with care; the third forbids the cord outright and instead defines the clearance around the unit.

None of this is this site's advice. It is each manufacturer's instruction, quoted from the manuals and linked per section, with the wattage rows taken from the dataset's published values.
