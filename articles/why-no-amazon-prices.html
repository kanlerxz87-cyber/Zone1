Why this site shows no Amazon prices — and why that is the point

Most affiliate pages show a price, a star rating and a review count. This one shows none of them, on purpose. The reason is a rule in Amazon's Program Policies, and the reason that rule exists is a good reason to keep reading a page like this one.

The rule, in Amazon's own words

Amazon's Program Policies govern what an Associate may publish. Two of the Participation Requirements cover the things you see on every other affiliate page in your feed:

Participation Requirement 2(b) permits displaying price and availability information only where Amazon serves the link or where the data comes through the official API. Requirement 6(t) restricts customer reviews and star ratings to content obtained through that same API S2.

The same paragraph carries a rule that bites affiliate comparison pages specifically: if you display prices for a product in any “comparison” format, together with prices for the same or similar products from other sources, “you must display both the lowest ‘new’ price and, if we provide it to you, the lowest ‘used’ price at which the Product is available on the Amazon Site” S2. That is why the comparison matrix on this site has no price column at all — a half-comparison is the thing the rule is aimed at.

That is why there is no Amazon price, no Amazon star rating and no Amazon review count anywhere on this site. It is not a stylistic choice and it is not an oversight. It is the program rules being followed.

The incentive structure follows from the same rule. If a commission rate only pays for a purchase, and the price on the page is whatever the page says, then a publisher has an incentive to quote a stale or wrong price — not to lie, usually, but to never update. A price that is allowed to update automatically from Amazon's own servers removes that incentive entirely. The same is true of ratings: a rating you typed yourself is an opinion, and a rating Amazon serves is data.

What this site shows instead

Where a manufacturer publishes a list price on its own product page, that figure appears here with a link to that page, and it is labelled as a manufacturer list price rather than as the price you will pay S10 S12 S14. Where a brand publishes a rating on its own site, that rating appears with its source and is labelled as a brand-site rating rather than as an Amazon rating S10 S12 S14.

Every one of these figures moves. A brand's own review widget moves with every sale, which is why the figures here are dated rather than presented as permanent. The honest version of a price is a price with a date on it.

The commission rate, stated honestly

Amazon's Standard Commission Income Statement publishes fixed rates by product category, and the table is public — no sign-in needed S3. Kitchen, Physical Books and Automotive earn 4.50%. Toys, Furniture, Home, Home Improvement, Lawn & Garden, Pets Products, Headphones, Beauty, Musical Instruments, Business & Industrial Supplies, Outdoors, Tools, Sports and Baby Products earn 3.00%. All Other Categories earn 4.00% S3.

What Amazon does not publish is the category assigned to a given ASIN. That means the rate for any specific product on this site is not knowable from the outside, so it is left blank rather than guessed at. Every figure on this site follows the same rule: published with its source, or blank and flagged.

How to check anything we claim

This is the part that matters more than the price. Every factual statement on this site carries a small source badge linking to the official page it came from — Amazon's own Associates Central documents, the US Federal Trade Commission's rule text, or the manufacturer's product page and owner's manual. Click any badge and you land on the document.

If a value could not be confirmed from an official source, it is not on the page in a filled-in form. It is on the Flagged for review page, with the reason and the evidence. That page currently lists every open irregularity in the project, including the ones we have not resolved.

The FTC's own guidance is the reason the rules are strict rather than decorative: the Endorsement Guides require that a material connection be disclosed clearly and conspicuously S8, and 16 CFR Part 255 is the binding rule that backs it S9. Amazon's own help page restates the requirement that the statement "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases." appear clearly and conspicuously on the site, and that the link-level disclosure be both clear — such as "(paid link)", "#ad" or "#CommissionsEarned" — and conspicuous, placed near the link rather than buried S5.

So the absence of a price on this page is not a gap in the research. It is the research, applied honestly.
