Compliance, in plain words

What 'As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases' actually obliges

The sentence in this site's header is a contract clause with a mandated wording and a placement rule. Who requires it, what it must look like, and the things it does not do.

By Zone1 Kitchen · AI-assisted document research· 5 min read· sources read 2026-11-14· Documentation-based — no hands-on testing Cluster: Can this page be trusted with my money?

No appliance was tested for this guide. Specifications are attributed to their documents; editorial guidance is not a cooking result. How we research.

The sentence is a contract clause, not a courtesy

Amazon's Associates Program Operating Agreement, section 5, requires it: 'You must clearly and prominently state the following … on your Site …: "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases."' The wording inside the quotation marks is the only disclosure text the agreement mandates — a site may not invent a prettier paraphrase and call it compliant.

The agreement makes the requirement load-bearing in section 6(a): 'any violation of Section 5 and as specified in the Program Policies will be deemed a material breach of this Agreement.' Material breach is the termination clause — the difference between a style critique and losing the account.

Sources for this section: S1

Where the sentence has to sit

Amazon's text sets a placement standard — 'clearly and prominently' — and the FTC's guidance for endorsements fills in what that means physically: the disclosure must be 'hard to miss' and 'placed with the endorsement message itself', not parked on an About page or at the end of a post where a reader has already made up their mind.

The codified rule underneath is 16 CFR §255.5, titled 'Disclosure of material connections'. An affiliate commission is a financial material connection — the exact situation the rule addresses — so the disclosure is not optional politeness; it is the mechanism the rule prescribes.

Sources for this section: S8 S9

The things the sentence does not do

It does not license untested claims. The FTC's companion rule is blunt: 'You can't talk about your experience with a product you haven't tried.' No product in this set was tested, so no article here narrates cooking results.

It does not unlock Amazon's data. Prices, availability, star ratings and review counts may be shown only when Amazon serves them or through the official API — the reason every price on this site is a manufacturer's list price or a blank cell.

It does not make the site Amazon's voice. The same section 5 forbids misrepresenting the relationship, 'including by expressing or implying that we support, sponsor, or endorse you'.

And it does not vary by page. A review, a comparison or a buying guide carries the same obligation as the homepage — the disclosure travels with the endorsement, per the FTC's placement rule.

Sources for this section: S8 S2 S1

How this site implements it

The sentence is emitted into the header of every page this site generates, above the first paragraph, and the site builder fails the build if a page ships without it. That is the practical answer to 'clearly and prominently': one fixed sentence, one fixed place, on every page, with the underlying documents linked from the compliance page.
