Compliance, in plain words

What does the Amazon Associate disclosure actually tell you?

It identifies a financial relationship. It does not prove a test, grant data rights or certify the site’s compliance.

By Zone1 Kitchen · AI-assisted, document-based writing· 3 min read· Sources checked 2026-11-19· Updated 19 Nov 2026 Documentation-based — no hands-on testing Cluster: Can this page be trusted with my money?

No appliance was tested for this guide. Specifications are attributed to their documents; editorial guidance is not a cooking result. How we research · first-publication timestamp is not established.

The required sentence

Amazon’s agreement and help require the Associate identification: “As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.” The publication repeats that exact sentence just below the masthead and in the footer.

Amazon’s disclosure help also calls for clear, conspicuous identification near affiliate links. Main product CTAs identify Amazon and the affiliate relationship beside the action.

Sources for this section: S1 S5

What a reader should not infer

A disclosure does not mean the author tried the product. FTC guidance says not to describe experience with something that was not tried; this publication discloses AI-assisted document research instead.

It also does not mean Amazon endorses the publication or that this particular account has earned a commission. Intended listing and enrolled-account attribution evidence remain unconfirmed.

Sources for this section: S8 S1 S41 S42

Content permissions are separate

Price/availability and reviews/star ratings have specific conditions in the Program Policies. This site has no configured authorised integration, and a disclosure does not change those conditions.

The layout/build checks can prove that the disclosure is present in the generated pages. They cannot award legal certification, account approval or a conversion result.
