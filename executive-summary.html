Findings

1 · The product side is verifiable, and was verified

Every specification asserted on the site was read from the manufacturer's own product page or owner's manual on 2026-11-13, and each one carries a clickable link to it. Twelve sources are used; none is a retailer, a review aggregator or an affiliate blog. Values that could not be confirmed are blank and listed, which is the only honest way to publish a comparison table.

2 · One official source contradicts another, and it is not cosmetic

The Chefman 8 Qt listing advertises a 450 °F Hi-Fry mode; the brand page and the RJ38-SQSS-8T manual document 200–400 °F. A buyer relying on the listing could receive a unit that does not match it. The site states both claims, asserts neither, and tells the reader to check the manual. Resolving this needs someone to open the live listing.

3 · The commission numbers cannot be stated per product, and pretending otherwise would be fabrication

Amazon's published rate table lists Kitchen at 4.50%, Home at 3.00% and a 4.00% fallback S3. Amazon does not publish which category a given ASIN sits in, and air fryers are not named in the table. Every "commission rate" figure on a competitor site therefore rests on a guess. This site shows the candidate rows and leaves the rate blank.

4 · The policy constraint is the architecture

The brief asks for automated product research, trend detection and optimisation. The IP License forbids aggregating, analysing, extracting or repurposing Program Content and forbids training models on it S2. So the automated research layer must run on manufacturer documentation and the operator's own click and conversion data. That is a harder, slower build — and the only one that survives review.
