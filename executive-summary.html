Findings

1 · The product side is verifiable, and was verified

Every specification asserted on the site was read from the manufacturer's own product page or owner's manual on 2026-11-13, and each one carries a clickable link to it. 34 sources are registered; none is a retailer, a review aggregator or an affiliate blog. Values that could not be confirmed are blank and listed, which is the only honest way to publish a comparison table.

2 · One official source contradicts another, and it is not cosmetic

The Chefman 8 Qt listing advertises a 450 °F Hi-Fry mode; the brand page and the RJ38-SQSS-8T manual document 200–400 °F. A buyer relying on the listing could receive a unit that does not match it. The site states both claims, asserts neither, and tells the reader to check the manual. Resolving this needs someone to open the live listing.

3 · The commission numbers cannot be stated per product, and pretending otherwise would be fabrication

Amazon's published rate table lists Kitchen at 4.50%, Home at 3.00% and a 4.00% fallback S3. Amazon does not publish which category a given ASIN sits in, and air fryers are not named in the table. Every "commission rate" figure on a competitor site therefore rests on a guess. What this site adds is evidence rather than a guess: on 2026-11-15 all three listings were re-read and all three render the Kitchen & Dining browse-node path, which makes 4.50% the well-evidenced working rate. The settlement-time category still comes only from the Earnings report, so both candidate rows stay on the page and the rate stays blank per product.

4 · The policy constraint is the architecture

The brief asks for automated product research, trend detection and optimisation. The IP License forbids aggregating, analysing, extracting or repurposing Program Content and forbids training models on it S2. So the automated research layer must run on manufacturer documentation and the operator's own click and conversion data. That is a harder, slower build — and the only one that survives review.

5 · Verifying the data and reading the page are different jobs, and only one of them was automated

Nine earlier passes checked every value against its source and found nothing wrong — correctly. But a value that is right in a table can still be wrong in a sentence, and eleven of them were: a published display label such as "Two 3-quart baskets" had been spliced into mid-sentence position, so the articles read as machine output ("splits a cavity into Two 3-quart baskets"). No verifier catches that, because the value is genuinely published. It took reading the rendered pages as a shopper would. The defect is fixed and the build now fails if a capitalised value is spliced into a mid-sentence position again S1.

Recommendation

  1. Publish this build. It satisfies the content substance a review looks for, and it is honest under inspection.
  2. Clear the flags that change copy first — F3 (the 450 °F conflict), F4/F5 (preset counts), F1/F2 (model mapping). Those decide what the pages may assert.
  3. Fix attribution before driving traffic — confirm the Associates tag and create sub-tags; F6 and F7.
  4. Then apply, and work the 180-day window deliberately S4.
  5. Only pursue price and rating display if API access is granted. Until then, absence is the compliant state.
  6. Decide what the AI agent is allowed to read before writing the research pipeline. The IP License forbids using Amazon's Program Content to build or improve models, and forbids aggregating or analysing it without written approval S2. The version of the brief that is lawful runs on manufacturers' own pages — which is the version this repository already implements. See SG-18.
  7. Decide the product set before the next content push. Two of the three products share a brand and a form factor, which caps what the comparison can answer. See SG-20.
