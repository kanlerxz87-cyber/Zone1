Most comparison tables hide gaps by quietly dropping the row. This one keeps every row and marks the gaps, because "we don't know" is a different statement from "it doesn't have it". Every filled cell links to the official page it came from.

The three real differences

Form factor

Two 3-quart drawers, one 8-quart basket, or a 26-quart oven cavity. Consider each compartment’s shape, not just the combined capacity. This is a buying question, not a cooking-performance test. S18 S12 S14

Documented ceiling

450 °F Hi-Fry for two minutes (Chefman 6 Qt manual S18), 400 °F (Chefman 8 Qt, per S12), 500 °F (Emeril, per S15). The 8 Qt's listing claims 450 °F — see the conflict note.

Information quality

Model-specific documents matter more than a familiar name. The 8 Qt temperature and dimension conflicts and the Emeril preset/accessory differences stay visible in the full product notes; none is resolved by this comparison. S12 S13 S14 S15 S29

Decision rules

  1. Need separate settings? The dual-basket manual documents independent time/temperature and Sync Finish. Check each basket’s usable dimensions; neither combined capacity nor Sync Finish guarantees food doneness. S18
  2. Feeding four to six people from one basket? The 8 qt single basket is the documented "4–6 people" unit S12.
  3. Replacing a toaster oven as well? The 26 qt oven-style documents rotisserie and toast, and it is the one whose footprint you should measure first (16.9 × 15.8 × 14.8 in S14).
  4. Worried about the 450 °F claim on the 8 qt? Read the manual that ships with the unit. This site will not resolve that conflict for you by guessing.
