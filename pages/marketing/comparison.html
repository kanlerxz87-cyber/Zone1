The short version

These three products are not really competitors — they are three answers to three different questions. If your question is "how do I cook two things at once?", only the dual-basket model answers it. If your question is "what is the best-value single air fryer for a normal household?", the 8-quart wins on verified price-to-demand. If your question is "what should I buy for a family of six?", the 26-quart French door oven is the only one that is big enough, and at $149.99 it is the only one that costs more than the other two combined.

Where the real differences are

Zones versus capacity. It is easy to assume that bigger is better. The verified data says otherwise: the 6 liters model does not beat the 8 quarts model because it is smaller, it beats it because it has two independent cooking zones with a synchronised finish. If you have never cooked a starter and a main simultaneously, that is the feature that changes your evenings.

Feature count versus usable space. The 26-quart unit advertises 24 cooking functions. The 8-quart unit advertises four. The 6-quart unit advertises a 200–450 °F range with a dedicated Hi-Fry setting. None of these numbers is directly comparable, and this page deliberately does not rank them — it reports what each manufacturer states and lets the reader's kitchen decide.

What we could not verify

The Emeril Lagasse 26QT French Door product page did not serve a readable specification block when we checked it, so this comparison publishes no dimensions, weight, wattage or material for it.
