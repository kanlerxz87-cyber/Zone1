Read the "matches" column carefully — three of them are not independent

IMPORTANT — the strength of the 'intended' column varies by row and the difference matters. For IR-01 to IR-03 the intended ASIN is the repository's existing candidate, which earlier passes derived by *following the same short links this pass observed*. So 'intended matches observed' on those three rows is not independent corroboration: it says the register agrees with a fetch, not that the owner has ever confirmed the model. That is exactly why confirmation_date is null on every row. For IR-04 and IR-05 the owner named no product at all, so the intended field is genuinely empty rather than merely unconfirmed.

Why intent and observation are two fields

Amazon defines an ASIN as the unique identifier of a listing, which is what makes an intended product checkable rather than remembered. If the observed target were written into the intended field, the record would assert a match nobody checked. A blank field below means unknown; it is never filled from a neighbouring field.

Rules this record follows

  • intended_asin, intended_listing_url and intended_source are transcribed from the owner's brief and are not evidence of anything working.
  • observed_redirect_target and observed_listing_title are what a fetch actually returned, with the date, and are not evidence of what the owner wanted.
  • confirmation_date stays null until the owner confirms the match from a logged-in browser. No session may fill it on the owner's behalf.
  • a blank field means unknown. It is never inferred from a neighbouring field.
