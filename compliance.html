Amazon Associate and FTC requirements, quoted from the source documents.

Links are not yet tagged. No Associates tag is configured, so no outbound link on this site can currently earn a commission. Set associate_tag in data/config.json and rebuild. Tracked as F-10.

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

I get commissions for purchases made through links on this page. Links below go to Amazon. Prices, ratings and availability on Amazon change; always confirm on the Amazon page before buying.

Twenty requirements, each read directly from Amazon's own program documents or from the FTC's published guidance. Every row quotes the operative sentence so you can check it yourself without trusting this page.

Two rules shaped this entire site more than any other. Amazon permits showing prices and availability only when Amazon serves them or they come through the Creators API (§2(b)), and permits showing Amazon reviews or star ratings only when they come through that API (§3(t)). Neither condition is met here. So this site shows no Amazon prices and no Amazon ratings — every such cell is blank, and each blank links to F-08. A third rule matters just as much: Amazon prohibits price-tracking and price-alert functionality (§3(y)), so none is built.

The disclosure, verbatim

Amazon's Operating Agreement §5 requires this exact sentence, or a substantially similar one previously allowed, to be stated clearly and prominently:

As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases.

It appears in the footer of every page and again in the disclosure panel above each product table. The FTC separately warns that “affiliate link” on its own is not an adequate disclosure, and that a single site-wide notice is not enough — so the plain-English sentence “I get commissions for purchases made through links on this page.” appears next to the links on every page, not only on the home page.

What is deliberately not built

Not built, and why

  • Amazon price tracking or price-drop alerts — prohibited by §3(y). Policies ↗
  • Amazon price and rating tables — §2(b) and §3(t) restrict this to API-served data. See F-08.
  • Cloaked or shortened outbound links — §3(v) requires the true destination to be visible. Every link here prints its full amazon.com/dp/ URL.
  • Click incentives, cashback or reward points — §3(g).
  • Amazon logos or brand imagery — the Trademark Guidelines restrict their use.
  • Third-party analytics, cookies or pixels — none are present, so the privacy page can make a clean claim.

Shipped instead

  • A verification pipeline where a table row cannot render without a source link.
  • A build-time assertion that rejects any outbound Amazon URL that is not a direct /dp/<ASIN> link.
  • Brand-store ratings, always labelled as brand-store and never as Amazon.
  • A tag-injection point that the build validates before use (F-10).
  • An explicit list of what is unverified, published rather than hidden.

Operator obligations that a website cannot enforce

Three duties sit with the operator, not the codebase. They are recorded here so they are not lost: never purchase through your own links, and never let relatives, employees or contractors do so (§3(u)); never offer any incentive for a click (§3(g)); and keep the site's original content genuinely original (§1).
