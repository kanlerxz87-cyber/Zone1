Owner review queue · 2026-11-19

Decide the 14 things that actually change the outcome.

This is the review surface the brief asked for: a single list of the decisions that change what the site can earn, what it may publish and what a reader sees. Each row states the question, what is already known from a named official source, what is missing, and the options. Nothing is implemented until you choose.

How to use it.
  1. Read the priority column from P0 down. P0 rows are the ones that make every other row either possible or pointless.
  2. A row marked 'needs your decision' cannot be completed by an agent: it requires an account, a credential, or a choice only the account holder can make.
  3. A row marked 'agent can proceed' has a defensible default. Quietly doing the default risks building the wrong thing, so it is tabled rather than pre-empted.
  4. Where a row cites S41 or S6, the answer to the attribution question is already documented. Read it before treating 'we do not know which link was intended' as a blocker.

DR-01 · P0 · Which Amazon listing is each supplied short link meant to point at? Needs your decision

The question. For each of the five supplied https://link.amazon/... strings, which exact 10-character ASIN is the product you intend a reader to buy?

What is already known. All five supplied short links were re-read live on 2026-11-19 and each reached an Amazon product page: B0CDY277D3 (Chefman 6 Qt dual basket), B09B7SB46R (Emeril 26QT French Door), B08DKYBTPH (Chefman 8 Qt TurboFry), B0DHZTCNR5 (ThermoMaven instant-read), B0DZFD6R9Z (CHEF iQ Sense Gen3). Those are observed destinations, not confirmed intents. S17 S28 S29 S36 S37 S41

What is still missing. The intended ASIN, the official listing URL, and the date and source of your confirmation. Without them, data/product-intent.json must leave four fields blank and every product page must carry 'listing unconfirmed'.

Why it matters. This single row converts 'observed' into 'confirmed' across five product records and removes the largest single caveat on every buyer page. It does not require a re-write: the field already exists and is designed for exactly this.

Cost of deciding: About ten minutes of your time; no engineering.

DR-02 · P0 · Is shopnow047c-20 an enrolled tracking ID, and may it be applied? Needs your decision

The question. Does shopnow047c-20 exist in your Associates account, and do you want it appended to the links readers actually click?

What is already known. The value is recorded as supplied. It has not been validated in Associates Central and does not appear on any supplied link. Amazon's own help states that the tracking ID does not need to appear in the URL for a sale to be tracked after the initial click, so a missing visible ID is not proof that attribution failed. S6 S41 S42

What is still missing. A screenshot-free confirmation that the ID exists in your account, and a decision to apply it. The repository keeps the operational tag blank by design until then.

Why it matters. Amazon's tracking-ID help documents that separate IDs can be reported separately, which is the only lawful first-party way to learn which page earns. Without it there is no measurement path at all.

Cost of deciding: Minutes in Associates Central.

DR-03 · P0 · Do you want to meet Amazon's qualifying-sales requirement in 180 days? Needs your decision

The question. An Associates account must make at least three qualifying sales within its first 180 days or it is closed, and the application is reviewed. Are you prepared to drive qualifying traffic inside that window?

What is already known. Amazon's Application Review page states the three-in-180-days requirement, a recency and follower expectation, and that a rejected application is final. This is a business fact that decides whether the whole project has a runway. S4 S1

What is still missing. Your answer. If the window is short, the site should chase the highest-intent product immediately rather than build breadth.

Why it matters. It determines the content strategy: chase one converting product, or build authority across a category. Those are different sites.

Choose a path (stored in this browser only; nothing is sent)

Cost of deciding: A decision, not a task.

DR-04 · P1 · Turn on the analytics collector? Needs your decision

The question. The self-hosted, privacy-preserving collector is implemented and tested but not deployed. Do you want it enabled, and do you accept the privacy obligations that come with it?

What is already known. The browser client and the Python/SQLite collector share a tested named-event contract covering page_view, outbound_click, article_engagement and comparison_interaction, plus an operator-only affiliate_report_import. It stores daily aggregate counts only. GitHub Pages cannot execute it, so a separate host is required. S2 S45

What is still missing. A host, and an actual privacy decision. No traffic figure, conversion or revenue number can be produced until this is live and receiving events.

Why it matters. Every 'measure and iterate' promise on this site is blocked here. The code exists; the deployment and the consent decision do not.

Choose a path (stored in this browser only; nothing is sent)

Cost of deciding: A host and about an hour of setup; ongoing privacy diligence.

DR-05 · P1 · Which three or four comparison attributes should be authoritative? Needs your decision

The question. The comparison table can grow any number of columns. Which few actually help a buyer, and which are noise?

What is already known. The manufacturer documents a large set: capacity, temperature range, dimensions, weight, power, safety approval and warranty. A reader deciding between a dual basket and an oven does not weigh all of them equally. Prior passes have published every row they could source, which is complete and also unreadable. S10 S12 S14

What is still missing. Your judgement of your reader. This is the one thing research cannot supply.

Why it matters. A shorter, opinionated comparison is the difference between a data dump and a decision tool. The long tables already exist on data.html.

Choose a path (stored in this browser only; nothing is sent)

Cost of deciding: One editorial choice; about an hour of page work.

DR-06 · P1 · Keep the 'no hands-on testing' position, or plan real testing? Needs your decision

The question. The site's honesty rests on disclosing that no product was handled. Do you want to keep that boundary, or fund real tests?

What is already known. The FTC's Disclosures 101 is explicit: you cannot talk about your experience with a product you have not tried. Every page currently discloses AI-assisted document research and no hands-on testing, which is truthful. Adding invented experience would not be. S8 S9

What is still missing. A decision, and if testing, actual access to the products and a method worth trusting.

Why it matters. A hands-on review is the strongest possible differentiator in this category, and the one thing a generated site cannot fake. If you can test, the site changes category entirely.

Choose a path (stored in this browser only; nothing is sent)

Cost of deciding: From zero to the cost of three appliances and your time.

DR-07 · P1 · Original product photography, or remain image-light? Needs your decision

The question. Do you have or can you produce your own product photographs?

What is already known. Amazon's Operating Agreement treats images as Program Content, and the site currently publishes only an AI-generated decorative scene, clearly labelled as not a product image. There is no authorised Amazon image integration configured, and copied photography is explicitly ruled out. S1 S2

What is still missing. Either your own photographs or a decision to stay with decorative imagery and typography.

Why it matters. Images are the largest perceived-quality gap between this site and a professional affiliate site. The gap cannot be closed with borrowed or generated product pictures without breaking a stated rule.

Choose a path (stored in this browser only; nothing is sent)

Cost of deciding: Photography time and equipment, or nothing.

DR-08 · P2 · What should the brand be called? Needs your decision

The question. The site currently publishes as 'Zone1 Kitchen', which is the repository name, not a chosen brand. What should it be called?

What is already known. The brand name appears in the masthead, page titles, structured data, the RSS feed and every canonical URL. It is cheap to change once and expensive to change after search engines and readers have learned it. S45

What is still missing. Your chosen name. This is a naming decision, not a research one.

Why it matters. A brand that reads like a placeholder undercuts every page's trustworthiness, and search engines reward a stable identity.

Cost of deciding: Minutes to choose, an hour to apply.

DR-09 · P2 · What is the publishing cadence, and who approves each guide? Needs your decision

The question. Should the site publish on a schedule, and does each new guide need your approval before it goes live?

What is already known. There are 25 published guides in the register, question-shaped, each sourced. A cadence and an approval step are editorial policy, not facts that can be researched. S43 S45

What is still missing. The cadence and the approval rule. Without them, 'publish regularly' is a slogan.

Why it matters. The brief asks the next topic to be chosen from measured queries. That only works if there is a rhythm and an owner for each decision.

DR-10 · P2 · Should the two thermometer guides expand into a full second category? Agent can proceed

The question. The two thermometer products now have buyer pages. Should the site build the matching guides, or keep thermometers as a narrower shelf beside the air fryers?

What is already known. Both manufacturer pages state a specification set worth writing about, including two real internal contradictions (read time, sensor count). The air-fryer side already has 25 guides; the thermometer side has two product pages and no guides. S39 S40 S48

What is still missing. An editorial appetite for a second category. The facts are already sourced.

Why it matters. Two supplied products are thermometers, and they are currently the thinner half of the site.

DR-11 · P2 · May the historical build directories be retired from the published site? Needs your decision

The question. The repository publishes several parallel builds (dashboard, s2, site, docs, annex, session pages) alongside the canonical publication. They are noindexed but publicly reachable. Keep them, or retire them from the deployment?

What is already known. Annex pages carry noindex,follow and an absolute canonical. They contain earlier, in some places contradicted, conclusions, though two of the most misleading statements were corrected on 2026-11-19. Anyone who browses them will meet older conclusions. S2

What is still missing. Whether you want the audit trail public. It is a policy decision with no research component.

Why it matters. A reader who lands on an annex gets a different, sometimes contradictory, story about the same products.

Cost of deciding: Low: exclude the directories from the Pages upload.

DR-12 · P3 · Publish the two USDA temperature minimums as a standing reference? Needs your decision

The question. Should the site publish a small, permanent 'safe minimum internal temperatures' reference, sourced to USDA, as a useful page in its own right?

What is already known. USDA FSIS and FoodSafety.gov both publish the figures, and they are already cited on the two new thermometer pages. Republishing a short table with attribution is a service to the reader and a genuine search entry point. S48 S49

What is still missing. Nothing factual. The question is whether you want the site to make that kind of public-service commitment.

Why it matters. It is the clearest example of content that is useful whether or not anyone buys anything, which is what separates a publication from a link farm.

Cost of deciding: About an hour.

DR-13 · P3 · Collect reader emails? Needs your decision

The question. Should the site offer email capture yet?

What is already known. There is a real RSS feed and no email service. The site has deliberately not built a fake signup. Email capture requires a provider, a privacy notice and a consent design. S45

What is still missing. A provider and a decision. Without them the RSS feed remains the only honest subscription.

Why it matters. Email is the only channel you own if search rankings move. Adding a pretend one is worse than having none.

Choose a path (stored in this browser only; nothing is sent)

Cost of deciding: A provider account and ongoing compliance.

DR-14 · P3 · What counts as evidence for the word 'trending'? Needs your decision

The question. The brief asks for trending products. What dated, official evidence would justify the word on any page?

What is already known. A dated snapshot of an official Amazon category page is the kind of evidence that could support such a claim. The site currently publishes no 'trending' or popularity claim anywhere, deliberately. S2 S43

What is still missing. A rule you are willing to be held to: which source, how dated, and what would make a claim expire.

Why it matters. The brief wants catchy, trendy topics. Catchy is achievable honestly; 'trending' without a dated source is not.
