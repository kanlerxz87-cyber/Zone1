Suggestions, and a place to choose between them

Sixteen things this pass believes would materially improve this site, in priority order. Nothing on this page has been implemented. Each entry names the problem it fixes, the official document or repository evidence behind it, two or three paths you could take, and the path this pass would pick if it were your call. Read the one you care about, choose a path, and the work is a short story rather than an open-ended project.

How to use this page. Nothing on this page has been implemented. It is a decision queue for the site owner. The score is priority = impact (1-5) x confidence that the evidence supports it (1-5). Both are this pass's judgement. The evidence links are the facts; the score is the opinion. Suggestions blocked by something only you can supply link to the exact row on the Open questions page.

The queue

SG-01 · Product identity

Settle which exact model each supplied link points to, before any more content ships

25priority

The problem. The site markets three products but cannot state which manufacturer model each Amazon listing is. Chefman publishes three 6-quart dual-basket models (RJ38-3DBW-V2, RJ38-SQPF-3TDB-2, RJ38-SQPF-3TDB-2W) and the 8-quart ASIN carries a 450 F Hi-Fry claim that the RJ38-SQSS-8T manual does not support. A buyer who lands on the wrong spec table is the one failure mode that costs both trust and money.

Evidence. Chefman 6 Qt dual-window product page (model RJ38-SQPF-3TDB-2W) ↗ Chefman 6 Qt dual-basket product page (sister SKU) ↗ Chefman 8 Qt product page ↗ Root flag register, rows F2, F3, F32, F33 ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Read the model number off each live listing (5 minutes, no code)
    Effort S · risk none · reversible
    Open each of the three Amazon links, scroll to the Product information table, and read the 'Model Number' row. Paste the three strings into a reply. That single act closes F2, F3, F32 and F33 at once.
  2. B. Stop publishing per-model spec tables until A is done
    Effort M · risk medium · reversible
    Demote the three product pages to a comparison of confirmed features only, with the model line blank and flagged. Costs the site its differentiation but removes every wrong-model risk.
  3. C. Ask the manufacturer
    Effort M · risk low · reversible
    Chefman and Emeril Everyday support can confirm which SKU an ASIN maps to. Slower than A and usually less authoritative than the listing itself.

This pass would pick: A, then keep publishing.

Why. Amazon does not publish ASIN-to-model mappings anywhere machine-readable, and the brand sites publish model numbers independently of the listing. The two only meet inside the listing's own Product information block, which is visible to you in a browser and to nobody without one. Every other route is inference, and inference is exactly what this project refuses to publish.

Cost of not doing it. High. A wrong spec table is a factual misstatement about a product you are paid to recommend, and it is the kind of error that attracts both refunds and FTC attention.

SG-07 · Compliance guardrail

Do not build price tracking or price alerts, whatever the brief asks for

25priority

The problem. The brief asks for a platform that 'analyzes pricing' and 'continuously optimizes campaigns'. Program Policies section 6(y) states that, unless otherwise agreed by Amazon, your Site must not have price tracking and/or price alerting functionality. A price-history chart or a 'notify me when it drops' feature is the most natural thing to build next and it is expressly prohibited.

Evidence. Program Policies, section 6(y) ↗ Platform blueprint page, guardrails section ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Write the prohibition into the build as a failing test
    Effort S · risk none · reversible
    A CI check that fails if a price-history, price-alert or chart component ever appears in a generated page or a data file. The rule then cannot be broken by accident in a later pass.
  2. B. Document it and rely on reviewers
    Effort none · risk medium · reversible
    A note in the platform page. Costs nothing and fails the first time an enthusiastic session does not read it.
  3. C. Request Amazon's written agreement
    Effort L · risk low · reversible
    Section 6(y) says 'unless otherwise agreed by Amazon', so a written exception is theoretically available. Realistically not granted to a new account.

This pass would pick: A. Make the policy executable.

Why. This repository already proves the technique works: build.py refuses to publish a spec row that has a value but no source. The same mechanism should refuse to publish a price-history feature. A policy that lives only in prose gets broken by the next well-meaning pass.

Cost of not doing it. High. Price tracking is the most commonly requested affiliate feature and the most clearly prohibited one here.

Blocked by: Nothing blocks this.

SG-02 · Attribution and revenue

Confirm the Associates tag is actually inside the three supplied links

20priority

The problem. The three links are link.amazon short links. The Associates tag (the 'tag=xxxxx-20' parameter) is inside the redirect and cannot be observed from outside. If the tag is missing, wrong, or belongs to a different account, every click on this site earns nothing, and nothing on the site would reveal it.

Evidence. Program Policies, section 2(a): Special Links must use the Associates ID assigned to you ↗ Program Policies, section 2(b): the tag must appear as a parameter in each link to an Amazon Site ↗ Flag F6, root register ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Open each link while signed in to Associates Central and watch the click register
    Effort S · risk none · reversible
    Associates Central reports clicks per tag in near-real time. One click on each link, then check Reports > Link Type. Confirms tag presence and ownership together.
  2. B. Replace the short links with full amazon.com URLs carrying your tag explicitly
    Effort S · risk low · reversible
    Rebuild each link as https://www.amazon.com/dp/ASIN?tag=YOURTAG-20 and read the tag back on the page. Makes the tag visible in the HTML, which is auditable and satisfies section 2(b) on its face.
  3. C. Keep the short links and hope
    Effort none · risk high · reversible
    No action. The revenue risk is unquantifiable and unmonitored.

This pass would pick: B, with A as the confirmation step. Replacing the short links also removes the link-shortening question in section 6(w) entirely.

Why. Section 6(w) forbids link shortening 'in a manner that makes it unclear that you are linking to an Amazon Site', and link.amazon keeps the Amazon name so it is probably compliant - but 'probably' is doing the work. A full amazon.com URL with a visible tag is unambiguous on both section 2(b) and 6(w), and it makes the single most commercially important fact on the site verifiable from the page source.

Cost of not doing it. Critical. Every other improvement on this page is worthless if the clicks do not credit your account.

SG-03 · Trust and compliance

Say who wrote each page and how it was made - and stop calling the site 'independent'

20priority

The problem. The site presents itself as 'an independent affiliate publisher' while monetising every product page. 16 CFR 465.6 makes it deceptive for a business to materially misrepresent that a website it controls provides independent reviews or opinions. Separately, the pages do not say that the analysis is documentation-based and machine-assisted, which a reader will assume is hands-on testing unless told otherwise.

Evidence. 16 CFR 465.6 - company-controlled review websites ↗ 16 CFR 255.5 - disclosure of material connections ↗ FTC Endorsement Guides Q&A ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Replace 'independent' with a precise, verifiable claim
    Effort S · risk none · reversible
    'Every specification on this page is quoted from the manufacturer or the US Code of Federal Regulations. We do not do hands-on testing and we say so on every product page.' Same pride, no misrepresentation.
  2. B. Add an About page naming the method and the author
    Effort S · risk none · reversible
    One page: who runs the site, how products are chosen, that AI assists with drafting, how corrections are handled, and how the site earns money. Link it from the footer of every page.
  3. C. Leave the framing and rely on the disclosure bar
    Effort none · risk high · reversible
    The Amazon disclosure sentence stays in the bar. The 'independent' claim and the missing method statement stay too.

This pass would pick: A and B together. This is the cheapest trust gain available on the site.

Why. The current copy is already honest about not testing - it just buries that in the footer instead of carrying it as the site's actual selling point. 'We quote the manual, page and section' is a stronger differentiator than 'independent', which is a word every affiliate site uses and which now carries regulatory weight under 16 CFR 465.6.

Cost of not doing it. Medium-high. The word 'independent' next to affiliate links is the specific fact pattern 465.6 was written for.

Blocked by: Q-09

SG-06 · Catalogue data

Publish no Amazon price or rating unless you hold Creators API access - and know which one you are choosing

16priority

The problem. The site displays no Amazon price, availability, star rating or review count. That is deliberately correct: Program Policies section 2(b) permits price and availability only when Amazon serves the link or when the data comes from Creators API or PA API, and section 6(t) restricts customer reviews and star ratings to API-served content. But it leaves a comparison table with a 'not displayed' column, which is a weaker buying aid than readers expect.

Evidence. Program Policies, section 2(b) (price and availability) and 6(t) (reviews and star ratings) ↗ Creators API / PA-API 5 deprecation notice ↗ Article explaining the policy to readers ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Ship compliant price via the Amazon-served link, and nothing else
    Effort M · risk low · reversible
    Use Amazon's own link widgets, which are served by Amazon and therefore permitted under 2(b). No API keys needed. The price is rendered by Amazon, not scraped by you.
  2. B. Apply for Creators API and render price and rating from it
    Effort L · risk medium · reversible
    Unlocks section 2(b) and 6(t) properly, including star ratings. Requires an approved Associates account, API credentials, and a small server - the site is currently static.
  3. C. Keep the current 'not displayed' column
    Effort none · risk low · reversible
    Fully compliant and honest, but the comparison table stays materially less useful than a competitor's.

This pass would pick: A now, B only if the account is approved and you want ratings.

Why. Option A gets a live price on the page without touching a policy line, because Amazon serves the data. Option B is the real prize - ratings are what most buyers scan - but it needs credentials this session does not have and a host that can keep a secret, which a static GitHub Pages site cannot.

Cost of not doing it. Medium for conversion. Do not fix it by scraping - that breaches section 2(b) and the IP License at once.

SG-08 · Measurement

Add click and conversion measurement that does not breach the privacy disclosure

16priority

The problem. The site currently has no analytics at all - the footer states it sets no cookies and runs no analytics, which is honest and satisfies Program Policies section 3(e) as written. The consequence is that no claim about which page converts can be tested. Any analytics added must be accompanied by an updated section 3(e) disclosure on the same commit.

Evidence. Program Policies, section 3(e) - cookie and third-party technology disclosure ↗ Current footer, which asserts no analytics are used ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Associates Central reporting only
    Effort S · risk none · reversible
    Amazon already reports clicks and orders per tracking ID. Use sub-tags (section 2(b) permits additional sub-tag IDs) to separate traffic sources. No third-party script, no cookie, no disclosure change needed.
  2. B. Add a cookieless, privacy-first analytics script
    Effort M · risk medium · reversible
    Server-side or cookieless page-view counts, no cross-site tracking, DNT respected. Requires a cookie/privacy disclosure update and a GDPR/PECR review for EU visitors.
  3. C. Add full GA4 with goal events
    Effort M · risk medium · reversible
    Richest data. Also the largest disclosure burden, sets cookies, and triggers consent-banner requirements in the EU and UK.

This pass would pick: A first - it is free, requires no disclosure change, and answers 'which page gets clicks' for the only metric that pays. Move to B only if you need on-site behaviour.

Why. Option A uses a mechanism Amazon already provides and explicitly permits. It also keeps the site's strongest trust claim - no tracking at all - true for longer, and that claim is worth more on a page whose whole pitch is that it does not mess with you.

Cost of not doing it. Medium. Without measurement, every content decision stays a guess.

SG-14 · Automation

Keep automated drafting, but make the claim-check gate the thing you trust

16priority

The problem. The brief asks for content generation at scale. That is achievable here - build.py already refuses to publish an article whose numbers do not appear verbatim in the verified dataset. The risk is the gate being treated as a formality rather than the product, and the IP License forbidding Program Content from being used to train or fine-tune models without approval.

Evidence. Program Policies, IP License and section 5 - no ML training on Program Content without approval ↗ Program Policies, section 3(c) - you are responsible for the accuracy of all material on your site ↗ Flag F11 - the platform features that need credentials ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Generate drafts from manufacturer documents only, and gate every number
    Effort M · risk low · reversible
    The model may restructure sentences; it may not introduce a value. Every figure must match data/products.json. Program Content never enters the drafting context, which keeps section 5 and the IP License satisfied.
  2. B. Generate from the Amazon listings too
    Effort M · risk high · reversible
    Richer input, but listing text is Program Content. Using it to build or fine-tune a model needs Amazon's prior written approval under section 5.
  3. C. Write everything by hand
    Effort L · risk low · reversible
    Best quality per page, does not scale, and abandons the brief's automation goal.

This pass would pick: A, with the gate extended to reject any article sentence containing a number that no registered source supports.

Why. This is already 80 percent built and it is genuinely rare - the guard rejected 18 paraphrased values while the article set was being written. The remaining work is making the gate harder to bypass, not writing more prose.

Cost of not doing it. Medium-high. Scaled content without a numeric gate produces exactly the AI-slop outcome the brief objects to.

SG-17 · Search visibility / compliance exposure

Decide what happens to the eight superseded builds that are still published next to the live site

16priority

The problem. The live site is not one site. This repository publishes 106 HTML pages, and only 27 of them are the maintained build generated by build.py. The rest are seven earlier or parallel builds (dashboard/, annex-88769516/, session-3740f69d/, research/, s2/, site/, docs/), each with its own homepage, its own near-duplicate comparison of the same three products, and in two cases a visible Amazon price. That has two costs. Search: Google's published policy states the presumption that a page matches the overall quality of its domain, and that a review weighs whether 'the same identical or near-identical content appears on multiple other sites' (S30) — nine copies of the same comparison on one origin gives a crawler no signal about which one is maintained, which is why the canonical tag added in this pass (F45) matters. Compliance: Amazon's Program Policies §2(b) permit price display only when Amazon serves the link or the data comes from the Creators/PA API (S2), so any published copy that shows a price is an exposure the maintained build deliberately avoids.

Evidence. Google Search Central — spam policies (scaled content abuse, site reputation policy, link qualification) ↗ Google Search Central — qualify your outbound links to Google ↗ Amazon Associates Program Policies (price and review display limits, §2(b), §6(t)) ↗ The published site and its sitemap (27 canonical URLs) ↗ Flag register rows F44 and F45 ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Keep the parallel builds, but make the canonical build unambiguous
    Effort S · risk medium · reversible
    Keep every older build published for auditability, since several are cited as evidence by later flags, and rely on the canonical tags added this pass plus a clear link from previous-passes.html. Lowest effort, keeps the audit trail intact, and leaves the duplicate-content risk in place.
  2. B. Demote the superseded builds out of search without deleting them
    Effort M · risk low · reversible
    Add a noindex robots meta to each annex build's pages, so the evidence stays online and reachable but stops competing with the maintained site. Needs a one-line change in each annex's own generator, so it is a cross-session change and must be logged on the task board rather than made unilaterally. Removes the duplicate-content risk while preserving every citation.
  3. C. Remove the duplicate pages and keep only their evidence files
    Effort L · risk high
    Delete the HTML of the superseded builds and keep the JSON, Markdown and evidence files they produced, repointing flags that link to them at the repository instead of a live page. Cleanest search result, largest change, and it destroys published output that other sessions produced. Needs the owner's explicit decision.

This pass would pick: B, and do it before promoting the site anywhere.

Why. It resolves the search and price-display exposure in one move, keeps every older build citable as evidence — which several flags depend on — and does not delete another session's published work. A is too weak to fix the duplicate-content signal; C throws away an audit trail this project has spent several passes building. B is also the only option whose blast radius is a single meta tag per annex.

Cost of not doing it. Nine near-identical copies compete for the same queries while the canonical build carries the only complete verification layer, and at least one published copy shows prices the maintained build refuses to display.

Blocked by: Nothing blocks this.

SG-13 · Trust

Give the site a corrections log, and correct in public

15priority

The problem. This project's most distinctive behaviour is refusing to guess and publishing what it cannot confirm. Evidence. Re-verification ledger - the raw material for a corrections log ↗ Flag F14 - brand-hosted review counts that moved between days ↗ data/reverification.json - the ledger a corrections log would be generated from ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Add a dated corrections table, fed from the re-verification data
    Effort S · risk none · reversible
    One row per corrected value: what it said, what it says now, the source, the date, and the reason. Generated from data/reverification.json so it cannot drift from the ledger.
  2. B. Note corrections only in commit messages
    Effort none · risk low · reversible
    Auditable by a developer, invisible to a reader.

This pass would pick: A. It costs one table and it is the single most credible thing an affiliate site can publish.

Why. Volatile facts on this site include brand-hosted review counts that changed within 24 hours. A visible corrections log converts the site's biggest weakness - it is not first-hand - into its most defensible claim: you can check everything, including our mistakes.

Cost of not doing it. Low. Opportunity cost.

SG-09 · Content strategy

Turn the eight source-linked articles into a cluster with one job each

12priority

The problem. There are eight articles and they are good - genuinely specific, sourced, and free of the filler that makes affiliate content worthless. But they overlap: two compare dual-basket against other form factors, and none is written for a high-intent query like 'chefman 6 quart dual basket air fryer review'. Without a deliberate map, the next eight articles will overlap the same way.

Evidence. Article index ↗ Platform blueprint, content engine section ↗ data/articles.json - the eight articles and their sources ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. One intent map, one primary query per article, and a rule against duplicates
    Effort S · risk none · reversible
    Assign each existing article exactly one search intent (informational, comparison, or transactional). Publish the map. New articles may only target an unclaimed intent. Two articles on one query is a build failure.
  2. B. Let the articles grow organically
    Effort none · risk medium · reversible
    Publish whatever seems interesting and let search sort it out.
  3. C. Collapse to three long guides
    Effort M · risk medium · reversible
    One buying guide, one comparison, one product page per SKU. Fewer, deeper pages.

This pass would pick: A, keeping all eight articles. They are too good to delete and too overlapping to keep growing unchecked.

Why. The existing title of one article - 'What 450F actually means' - is exactly the kind of specific, answerable question that earns a link and a ranking. The fix is not more articles, it is a rule that stops the ninth from duplicating the fifth.

Cost of not doing it. Low-medium. Costs compounding, not immediate damage.

SG-10 · Economics

Stop presenting the commission rate as unknown where the category is knowable

12priority

The problem. All three products show commission as '- not confirmed -' with two candidate rows, Kitchen 4.50% and All Other Categories 4.00%. That is scrupulously honest, because Amazon publishes rates by category but not which category an ASIN sits in. It is also more cautious than the evidence requires for an air fryer, which Amazon files under Kitchen & Dining - a fact visible in the listing breadcrumb on every one of the three links.

Evidence. Standard Commission Income Statement, Table 1 - Kitchen 4.50%, All Other Categories 4.00% ↗ Chefman 6 Qt listing breadcrumb: Home & Kitchen > Kitchen & Dining > Small Appliances > Fryers > Air Fryers ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Record the breadcrumb as evidence and show Kitchen 4.50% as the working rate
    Effort S · risk low · reversible
    Capture the category path Amazon itself displays on each listing, cite it, and show 4.50% as the rate with the breadcrumb as the source. Keep the fallback row visible in a footnote.
  2. B. Keep both candidate rows until Associates Central confirms the category
    Effort S · risk none · reversible
    Consulting Reports > Orders, or the Earnings report line item, shows the category Amazon actually applied. Definitive; needs account access.
  3. C. Leave it as 'not confirmed'
    Effort none · risk low · reversible
    Defensible, and it costs the reader nothing - but it makes the economics section unusable for deciding whether to invest in this niche.

This pass would pick: B, falling back to A if the account has no earnings history yet.

Why. A rate at 4.50% versus 4.00% moves the break-even traffic estimate by about 12 percent, which is material to whether the niche is worth the effort. The category is not actually unknowable - Amazon shows it in the breadcrumb and applies it in Reports. Recording 'unknown' when a source exists is a different failure from refusing to guess, and the brief asks for decision-grade information.

Cost of not doing it. Low directly, medium for planning. You cannot size a niche whose unit economics you refuse to compute.

SG-11 · Programme viability

Check the account against the 180-day qualifying-sale threshold before investing in content

12priority

The problem. Amazon's application review requires qualifying sales within a set window, and an unapproved account earns nothing. The site is currently built as if the account is live. Whether it is, and how much of the window has elapsed, changes the entire plan: an account near the deadline needs conversion work, not more articles.

Evidence. Associates Central - application review process ↗ Operating Agreement, term and termination ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Read the status in Associates Central and record it here
    Effort S · risk none · reversible
    One line: approved, in review, or closed; and the date the current window started. Everything downstream can then be sequenced against a real deadline.
  2. B. Assume approved and optimise for revenue
    Effort none · risk medium · reversible
    Proceed as if clicks pay today.
  3. C. Assume unapproved and optimise for the sale count
    Effort M · risk low · reversible
    Prioritise the pages most likely to produce a first sale - the highest-intent product review - over broad informational articles.

This pass would pick: A, then C if the answer is 'in review'.

Why. These two worlds need opposite content. If the account is approved, breadth earns; if it is in review, one high-intent page that produces three sales is worth more than twenty explainers. This session could not read the threshold page's current wording, so the exact figures are flagged unverified rather than restated from memory.

Cost of not doing it. High if the account is unapproved and the window is running.

SG-16 · SEO foundation

Decide the domain question before investing in SEO

9priority

The problem. The site is served from kanlerxz87-cyber.github.io/Zone1/. That is a subdirectory of a shared github.io origin, which means its authority is partly shared with every other project on that account and its URLs are ugly to cite. Every inbound link earned today points at a URL you cannot easily migrate later.

Evidence. Current deployment URL ↗ GitHub Pages custom domain documentation ↗ robots.txt and sitemap.xml generated by build.py, both scoped to the github.io URL ↗

Paths you could take

  1. A. Register a domain and serve Pages from it
    Effort S · risk low · reversible
    CNAME to GitHub Pages, HTTPS enforced. Roughly the price of a coffee a month. Do it before the first backlink, not after.
  2. B. Keep github.io
    Effort none · risk medium · reversible
    Free, zero maintenance, and permanent - but weak for search and impossible to move without losing accumulated links.

This pass would pick: A, and do it early enough that the migration is trivial.

Why. The cost of moving is proportional to the links already pointing at the old address. That number is currently close to zero. Choosing to stay is a decision to be permanently second-class in search; choosing to move later is a decision to lose whatever was earned in the meantime.

Cost of not doing it. Medium, and it compounds in the wrong direction.
