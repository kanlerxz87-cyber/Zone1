How to choose an air fryer without being sold to

A form-factor decision dressed up as a shopping decision. Work down these five questions in order and most of the market falls away before you look at a single price.

Written for this product set. Every factual number links to the manufacturer's own page or manual; no product is claimed to have been tested.

2. Match capacity to the household, using published numbers

Quart figures are nominal cavity volume, not usable food volume, and no manufacturer in this set publishes a usable-volume figure. The only official household-size statement in the set is Chefman's "designed for 4–6 people" for the 8 qt S12. Treat every other capacity-to-people mapping you read online, including your own, as an estimate.

3. Check what the top temperature setting really does

The single most useful thing you can do before buying

Find the owner's manual and read the temperature range, not the box. A "450 °F" badge can mean a full-cycle setting, a two-minute finish mode, or nothing at all. In this product set the Chefman 8 Qt's Amazon listing advertises 450 °F Hi-Fry while its own manual documents 200–400 °F S12 S13 S16. That contradiction is unresolved and is flagged rather than smoothed over.

4. Check cleaning before you check features

All three units in this set document nonstick, dishwasher-safe baskets or racks S10 S12 S14. Where they differ is documented cleaning caveats: Chefman's manual warns specifically against aerosol spray on the nonstick coating S13. That is the kind of line that decides whether a coating survives a year.
